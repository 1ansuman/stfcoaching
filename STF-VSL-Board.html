Production guide

Format

Screen recording of a Miro board / Google Doc with Ben's face in a webcam circle. Classic free course format. Use OBS, Loom, or QuickTime.

Alternative: Ben on camera + text overlays in editing. Or mix both.

Audio

Lav mic or AirPods. Test audio before filming the whole thing. Film in a quiet room with soft surfaces.

Editing

CapCut, iMovie, or DaVinci. Add timestamps, subscribe animation at 5 min, overlay client screenshots when referenced. Lower-third @benzuckfit.

What to gatekeep

Give away 95%. Be open on principles, science, frameworks, mindset. Hold back: exact macro calculations, programming templates, daily check-in system, real-time adjustments.

The strategy

Teach the viewer so thoroughly they think: "this guy clearly knows what he's talking about, I need him to do this for me." The viewer gets knowledge. The client gets Ben.

Framework

8-part free course VSL structure: Hook → Opportunity → Roadmap → Step-by-step → Social proof → Objections → Reality check → CTA

1

Assessment

~2–3 min
Pillar 1

7-day baseline tracking

Document current reality for a full 7 days before changing anything. Cannot engineer a system without knowing the starting inputs.

  • Current weight (same conditions daily — after waking, before water)
  • Exact sleep hours (sleep regulates cortisol → fat storage)
  • Precise eating habits (record everything, change nothing yet)
  • Real activity level (honest — not idealized)

Common mistake

Lying to yourself about inputs. "I eat pretty clean" = fast food 4x/week. "I'm pretty active" = desk for 9 hours + walk to car. The plan only works if inputs are accurate.

2

Nutrition

~3–4 min
Pillar 2

The engineered deficit

  • Caloric deficit: 300–500 below maintenance
  • Protein = priority #1 (preserve lean mass in deficit)
  • Fill remaining calories with fats + carbs based on preference
  • Keep the foods you enjoy — no elimination diets
  • Consistency over perfection, always
  • Track exact macronutrient targets (not just "eating clean")
Gatekeep moment

"The exact macro calculations and the specific adjustments you would make week to week based on how your body is responding, that is where having a coach comes in. But the principle is what matters here. Small sustainable deficit. Protein high. Foods you enjoy. Consistency over perfection."

Common mistake

All-or-nothing thinking. One bad meal does not ruin a week of consistency. But one bad week can ruin a month if you let it snowball. Stay in the game.

3

Training

~2–3 min
Pillar 3

The 45-minute stimulus

  • 45 min sessions, 5 days/week
  • Push / Pull / Legs split
  • Compound movements as foundation
  • Every working set to physical failure (triggers adaptation)
  • Cardio = incline walking (supplement, not main driver)
  • Goal: build lean muscle → more calories burned at rest

Common mistake

Too much cardio, not enough weights. If you only have 45 minutes, spend 40 lifting and 5 warming up. Weights change body composition. Cardio is a tool on top.

4

Consistency systems

~2–3 min
Pillar 4

Put the protocol on autopilot

  • Train same time every day (remove the daily decision)
  • Meal prep same time every week
  • Track food, workouts, weight daily
  • Bad day ≠ skip. Bad day = 20-min backup workout
  • 50% effort beats 0% every time
  • What gets measured gets managed

Handle objections

~2 min
On camera. Direct and empathetic. Not arguing — removing their last excuses.
"I don't have time."
45 minutes = 3% of your day. You spend more than that scrolling. It's not about having time — it's about deciding what matters.
"I've tried before and it didn't work."
It didn't work because the plan wasn't built for you. A generic meal plan and a PDF workout is not coaching. That's a product.

You could, but you haven't. YouTube has everything for free. What you're missing isn't knowledge — it's someone making sure you follow through.
"I'm not that out of shape."
You don't need to be at rock bottom to get help. Most guys who get results are functional men who just know they're not where they want to be.
